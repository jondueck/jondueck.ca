New music discovery has come up as a topic of discussion a few times with different friends. I try to listen to lots of new music, so here’s what’s worked for me:

## Friends
I take recommendations from friends seriously, and try to be intentional with what music I recommend to which friends.

## Following music writers/blogs 
Chicago’s Josh Terry and his newsletter, [*No Expectations*](https://www.noexpectations.fyi) is a great example. I recently started following [Bad Gardening Advice](https://badgardeningadvice.com) and [Cups N Cakes](https://www.cupsncakespod.com/pick-of-the-week/) for more Canadian music.

## Labels
When you find an artist or album you like, check out other releases from the label.

## Musicians

Read the credits. See what else that pedal steel player has played on.

## Setting a goal

In recent years I’ve set loose goals for myself as a way to push myself to find new music. Last year I tried to listen to more Canadian music. This year it’s been trying to listen to music from different countries. One way I’ve done that is by looking at [Rate Your Music’s 2026 chart, but filtering out the USA and the UK](https://rateyourmusic.com/charts/top/album/2026/loc:-united%2dstates,-united%2dkingdom/).

## Listen to the Radio
I’ve been listening to two shows on [CKUW](https://ckuw.ca), the University of Winnipeg campus radio station: [Juliana Young](https://julianacyoung.com)’s folk show, [*Barking Dog*](https://julianacyoung.com/barkingdog), and [Jacob Brodovsky’s](https://www.jacobbrodovsky.com) interview show *Let’s Play DJ*.

## Take chances

See interesting album art? Take a listen. You might like it.
